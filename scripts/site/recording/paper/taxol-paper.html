The side chain of paclitaxel and the shapes it takes: a worked example

The Meno project
Abstract. Paclitaxel carries a flexible ester side chain at C13 on a rigid taxane core. How that chain folds has been discussed for decades, under names that describe its shape: extended, collapsed onto the core, or T-shaped. This note follows one routine path from the drawn structure to an answer at room temperature: a conformer search, an optimisation of each conformer, and the grouping of the results into families by the contacts that define them.

1. Introduction

Paclitaxel is a diterpenoid from the bark of the Pacific yew. Its tetracyclic core, with a four-membered oxetane ring and eleven stereocentres, made it one of the classic targets of total synthesis, and its first total syntheses were reported in 1994. The core is nearly rigid. The N-benzoyl-β-phenylisoserine ester at C13 is not: rotation about its single bonds lets its two aromatic rings reach towards, or away from, the benzoate at C2 and the acetate at C4.

Which of these shapes the molecule adopts, in solution and when bound, is a question about a family of structures, not about a single one. That makes it a good example of working with the whole conformer set.

2. Methods

A conformer set was generated from the drawn structure with a distance-geometry search, keeping every distinct conformer within 8 kcal/mol of the lowest. Each conformer was optimised with a semi-empirical tight-binding method, and duplicates were removed by their heavy-atom root-mean-square deviation (threshold 0.25 Å). Relative energies are electronic energies; populations follow from a Boltzmann distribution at 298.15 K.

Conformers were sorted into three families by two distances: from the centroid of the C3′ phenyl ring to that of the C2 benzoate, and to the methyl group of the C4 acetate. A conformer with the phenyl within 5 Å of the benzoate is collapsed; one with it near the acetate and away from the benzoate is T-shaped; the rest are extended.

3. Results

The search returned 63 conformers, which became 21 after optimisation and removal of duplicates. Table 1 gives the lowest conformer of each family; Figure 2 gives the population each family carries.

No family dominates. The collapsed conformers, in which the side-chain phenyl lies over the C2 benzoate, are lowest in energy, but the T-shaped and extended families together carry more than half of the population.

4. Discussion

A single optimised structure, taken from a drawing, would have landed in one of these families more or less by chance, and with it any conclusion drawn from its shape. The set shows instead that the side chain moves between three shapes separated by less than 1 kcal/mol, which is the more useful answer to the question asked.

Every step here is routine. What makes it worth writing down is that each step can be repeated and checked: the drawn structure, the search and its options, the optimisations and the energies belong together, and a reader can rerun them in order.

5. Conclusion

Paclitaxel's side chain spreads over collapsed, T-shaped and extended forms within a narrow range of energy. The example shows a complete line of work, from drawing to populations, on a molecule large enough for the answer to matter.
