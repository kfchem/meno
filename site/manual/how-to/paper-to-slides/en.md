# Take structures out of a paper and make slides of them

Read a paper beside your work, take two of its structures out onto the page as structures you can edit, give each a slide, and present them.

1. {#open} Press ⌘O (Ctrl+O on Windows), and choose the paper's PDF in the window that opens. It is put on the page.
2. {#read} Double-click the PDF. It opens in a column on the right of the page.
3. {#narrow} Drag the column's left edge to the right, to give the page more room.
4. {#take-first} In the column, point at a structure: it lights up. Drag it onto the upper left of the page. It is put down chosen, together with the PDF it came from.
5. {#take-second} Drag the next structure onto the page, beside the first.
6. {#choose-first} Press and hold the first structure to choose the whole of it, alone. As the pointer rests on it, it says where it came from.
7. {#menu-first} Right-click it.
8. {#slide-first} Choose *Slide around these*. A slide is drawn round the structure, with its number at its upper left.
9. {#choose-second} Press and hold the second structure.
10. {#menu-second} Right-click it.
11. {#slide-second} Choose *Slide around these*. A dashed arrow leads from the first slide on to the second. Neither the numbers nor the arrow show in the talk.
12. {#clear} Click empty space on the page, so that nothing is chosen.
13. {#present-menu} Right-click the same empty space.
14. {#present} Choose *Present*. The window fills the screen with the first slide. Press Escape to end the talk: the page comes back with that slide in view.
15. {#fit} Press ⌘1 (Ctrl+1 on Windows) to bring everything on the page into view.
