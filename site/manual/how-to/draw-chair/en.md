# Draw a chair with groups axial and equatorial

Put a cyclohexane chair on the page, add one group equatorial and one axial on the same atom, and flip the chair.

1. {#open-quick-add} Double-click an empty spot on the page. A row of icons opens.
2. {#projections} Choose *Projections*, the icon of a chair. A second row opens below it.
3. {#chair} Choose *Chair*. A chair is put down where you double-clicked.
4. {#equatorial} Double-click the chair's lower right end. A group is added at the atom's equatorial place, out to the side. As the pointer rests on the atom, a tip says *No R or S: two of its groups are the same.*
5. {#axial} Double-click the same atom again. A second group is added at its axial place, straight down.
6. {#flip-menu} Right-click an atom of the ring.
7. {#flip} Choose *Flip chair*. The ring flips: the group that was axial is now equatorial, the one that was equatorial is now axial, straight up, and each stays on its own face. The compound is the same.
8. {#flat-menu} Right-click an atom of the ring again.
9. {#flat} Choose *Draw flat*. The ring is laid out flat, its two groups drawn with a wedge and a hashed wedge.
10. {#undo} Press ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows). The chair comes back as it was.
11. {#let-go} Click an empty spot on the page. The chair is let go, and you see it as it is drawn.
