# Draw a chair with groups axial and equatorial

Put a cyclohexane chair on the page, add one group equatorial and one axial on the same atom and one on the atom beside it, and flip the chair.

1. {#open-quick-add} Double-click an empty spot on the page. A row of icons opens.
2. {#projections} Choose *Projections*, the icon of a chair. A second row opens below it.
3. {#chair} Choose *Chair*. A chair is put down where you double-clicked.
4. {#equatorial} Double-click the chair's lower right end. A group is added at the atom's equatorial place, out to the side.
5. {#axial} Double-click the same atom again. A second group is added at its axial place, straight down.
6. {#neighbour} Double-click the atom beside it, at the middle of the chair's lower edge. A group is added at that atom's equatorial place, and an *R* shows beside the atom.
7. {#flip-menu} Right-click an atom of the ring.
8. {#flip} Choose *Flip chair*. The ring flips: each group that was axial is now equatorial, and each that was equatorial is now axial, each on its own face. The compound is the same, and the *R* is the same.
9. {#flat-menu} Right-click an atom of the ring again.
10. {#flat} Choose *Draw flat*. The ring is laid out flat, its groups drawn with wedges and hashed wedges, the *R* still beside its atom.
11. {#undo} Press ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows). The chair comes back as it was.
12. {#let-go} Click an empty spot on the page. The chair is let go, and you see it as it is drawn.
