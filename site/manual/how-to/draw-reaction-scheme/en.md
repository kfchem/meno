# Draw a reaction scheme for a document or slides

Draw cyclohexanol, an arrow with PCC over it and CH2Cl2 under it, and cyclohexanone made from a copy of the first; give the page a journal's style, and copy the whole scheme to paste into a document or a slide.

1. {#open-quick-add} Double-click an empty spot in the upper left of the page. A row of icons opens.
2. {#chain} Choose *Chain*, the zigzag icon. A blue dot shows where the chain begins, with a faint lattice of hexagons round it.
3. {#ring} Move the pointer round one hexagon of the lattice, from corner to corner, clockwise, without pressing the button, and click where you began. The chain follows the pointer a bond at a time and closes a six-membered ring.
4. {#bond} Double-click the ring's top corner, a carbon atom. A bond is drawn out of it, straight up.
5. {#oxygen} Point at the new bond's free end, so that it lights up, and type O. It becomes OH: cyclohexanol.
6. {#arrow-quick-add} Double-click an empty spot to the right of the ring, level with its middle. The row of icons opens.
7. {#arrow} Choose *Arrow*. A reaction arrow is put down where you double-clicked.
8. {#over-quick-add} Double-click just above the middle of the arrow. The row of icons opens.
9. {#over-text} Choose *Text*, the T. A box for words opens above the arrow.
10. {#over-words} Type PCC.
11. {#over-enter} Press Enter. The words are kept, over the arrow.
12. {#under-quick-add} Double-click just below the middle of the arrow.
13. {#under-text} Choose *Text* again. A box for words opens under the arrow.
14. {#under-words} Type CH2Cl2. Each 2 is set lower by itself, as in a formula.
15. {#under-enter} Press Enter. The words are kept under the arrow, and the arrow grows to fit them.
16. {#choose} Press and hold a bond of the ring. After a moment the whole structure is chosen, shown in blue.
17. {#copy} Press ⌘C (Ctrl+C on Windows) to copy it.
18. {#paste} Point at the empty space to the right of the arrow, level with the first structure, and press ⌘V (Ctrl+V on Windows). The copy is put down there, the same size and level with the first, and stays chosen.
19. {#double} Double-click the copy's bond to its O. It becomes a double bond, and OH becomes O: cyclohexanone.
20. {#let-go-copy} Click an empty spot below the scheme. The copy is let go.
21. {#page-menu} Right-click the same spot. The page's menu opens.
22. {#styles} Choose *Styles…*. The *Styles* column opens at the right, and the page moves over to make room for it.
23. {#acs} Choose *ACS 1996*. The page is drawn at once in that journal style: its letters - OH, O and the words on the arrow - change to the style's typeface.
24. {#close-styles} Close the column with the cross at its top right. The page moves back.
25. {#choose-everything} Press ⌘A (Ctrl+A on Windows). Everything on the page is chosen.
26. {#copy-everything} Press ⌘C (Ctrl+C on Windows). The whole scheme is copied: paste it into a document or a slide, where it goes in as a drawing.
27. {#let-go} Click an empty spot on the page. Nothing is chosen, and you see the scheme as it is drawn.
